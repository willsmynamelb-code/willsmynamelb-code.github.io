THE FIRST WEEK DAY 1 OF 7
JANUARY 1 · STEP ONE

You Have Arrived

Sometimes a new beginning looks just like an ordinary morning.

Sunlight crosses the kitchen table. Outside, a truck shifts gears and moves on. Everything looks familiar, but this day has never been lived.

You may have called this day one before. You may be tired of promises, especially your own. Yet your willingness to try today matters.

Place your feet on the floor. Feel what holds you steady. Take one unhurried breath. You are here.

Step One begins with honesty about what addiction has cost and what you haven't been able to control. The truth may ache, but seeing it clearly is not the same as condemning yourself. It gives you somewhere real to begin.

Outside, the morning continues, and you can meet it one honest step at a time.

Today's Practice

Find one quiet minute. Notice your feet on the floor. Name one truth about your recovery, then choose one small way to honor it.

Ask Yourself

What truth could help me take my next step?

Carry This With You

This day is still yours to live.

JANUARY 2 · STEP ONE

You Are Not Every Thought You Think

A thought can be loud without being true.

You’ll never stay sober. You always wreck it. Everybody knows it, and honestly, so do you.

The voice has your history memorized, and it talks in your own accent, which is why it sounds so much like the truth.

Listen closely. It says always and never as if it knows your future. It says everybody as if it has asked them. And it leaves out every morning you got up anyway.

You don’t have to shout it down; arguing only gives it the floor. Try putting five words in front of it instead: I’m having the thought that I’ll never stay sober. The sentence is the same, but now you’re standing a step back from it. Something in your chest may loosen a notch and let the next breath go deeper.

The voice will probably be back tomorrow. So will you.

Today's Practice

When the old voice starts up today, repeat its sentence with five words in front: I’m having the thought that…

Ask Yourself

Which recurring thought have I accepted without examination?

Carry This With You

You are the listener, not every voice you hear.

JANUARY 3 · STEP ONE

You Are Not the Worst Thing You Did

A mistake can be part of your story without defining who you are.

The woman in the third folding chair has her grocery-store badge clipped to her coat. It shows her first name. For eleven winters she called herself something else, under her breath: “thief.” Anything decent she did got filed as a thief being nice.

Two years ago, someone in this circle asked her to say what she had done in one sentence, no labels. Only what happened. She told it to the floor: “I took money from my mother’s purse, more than once, and let my brother take the blame.”

She hadn't excused what she'd done. She was learning to carry the truth without wearing it as her name.

She can say it now without looking down, though her face still goes hot. The radiator knocks. She stirs too much sugar into a paper cup and waits for the next person to talk.

Today's Practice

When an old name for yourself returns, be still a moment. Remember what happened without forgetting that there is more to you.

Ask Yourself

What can I take responsibility for without condemning myself?

Carry This With You

Repair is more truthful than endless self-punishment.

JANUARY 4 · STEP ONE

The Freedom of Beginning Again

Beginning again doesn't erase yesterday. It changes what you do today.

A hardware-store calendar hangs by the kitchen door. Pencil X’s mark the first two days of January. The third square is empty.

You planned to attend a recovery meeting each evening. You went the first two nights. Yesterday, tired and unsure what to say, you stayed home.

Morning light falls across the calendar. Your eyes find the empty square, as though it cancels the others.

But those first two nights happened. You showed up. You listened. You sat among people who understood.

Stand quietly. Let your shoulders settle. Acknowledge why yesterday was difficult without judging yourself.

The fourth square is empty too. The day has barely begun. You can ask for support and return to the meeting tonight. There's room for another X.

The calendar hangs a little crooked on its nail.

Today's Practice

Before noon, call or text one safe person. Tell them how you're doing and what support you need today.

Ask Yourself

What has helped me before that I can return to today?

Carry This With You

A new beginning is a different response, not a spotless past.

JANUARY 5 · STEP ONE

Stop Abandoning Yourself

Your yes should be a choice, not a reflex.

At your cousin’s birthday, the coats are piled on the bed and the music keeps getting louder.

When you finally stand, she catches your sleeve. “You’re leaving already? It’s not even ten.” You hear yourself say, “Okay, one more hour,” and sit back down.

That yes was out before you checked it. Maybe you’ve done it for years, keeping the peace and swallowing what you needed. It kept you safe once. You can be grateful for that and still put it down.

The bass comes up through the floor into your feet. Let a breath go all the way out.

Honesty doesn’t have to be harsh. “I need to head home. Happy birthday.” Her face may fall, and you can care about that and still go.

You dig your coat out from the bottom of the pile.

Today's Practice

Before you say yes to anything today, wait one breath and ask yourself, quietly: Do I mean this?

Ask Yourself

When did I say yes because I feared disappointing someone?

Carry This With You

Love should make room for both people.

JANUARY 6 · STEP ONE

The Courage to Feel

You can feel something fully without letting it decide for you.

It’s after two in the morning, and the feeling you’ve been stepping around has found you, with nothing between you and it.

You know what the old way offered on nights like this. You don’t have to do anything with this feeling except have it.

Let it be the size it is, and give it a plain name: sad, scared, furious, ashamed, lonely.

Find where it lives, maybe a weight under the ribs, and rest a hand there. A hand fixes nothing and keeps you company anyway.

A feeling may have something to tell you without telling the whole truth. Anger may rise when you're hurt. Fear may be an old hurt returning. It could still be here at dawn, in a different shape.

Across the city, a few other windows are lit.

You are allowed to be awake.

Today's Practice

When a hard feeling rises today, name it and give yourself sixty seconds before deciding what to do next.

Ask Yourself

What might this feeling be telling me, and what might it not know?

Carry This With You

Listen to the feeling, then question its orders.

JANUARY 7 · STEP ONE

You Need Not Earn Your Right to Be Here

Worth is not a reward for getting everything right.

Some of us came into recovery with a list ready, the jobs we used to hold and the most sober days we ever strung together. We thought we had to prove we were worth helping.

Nobody asked for the list.

The first Step asks for one honest admission: we couldn’t get out of our addiction on our own. Saying it out loud felt like giving up our last bit of pride. But our dignity was still there.

Worth that has to be earned can be lost on a bad day, and we have bad days. We are still responsible for what comes next, and the work is hard. But we were worth helping before any of it began.

Today's Practice

Today, think of one thing you've been trying to handle alone. Ask someone you trust for a little help, without feeling you have to prove you deserve it.

Ask Yourself

Whose approval am I still trying to earn?

Carry This With You

Growth starts from the dignity you already have.

The purpose behind T.Y.M.E.

Today is where change begins.

We cannot change yesterday, but we can choose how we meet today. T.Y.M.E. brings honest reflection, responsibility, compassion, and connection to people navigating recovery and everyday life.

We don't deny the past. We learn from it. We don't ignore the future. We prepare for it.

About the founder

T.Y.M.E. was founded by William Lee Meyers, who writes as William Meyers. His lived experiences with addiction and mental health challenges helped shape this project and the book A Sober Companion.

Meet the Author on Amazon

Looking ahead

The Vision

My vision for T.Y.M.E. (Today You Mean Everything) is to help create a world where no one is defined by their past, their struggles, or their mistakes.

T.Y.M.E. began with books inspired by lived experience and a simple belief: meaningful change can begin today. But the vision extends far beyond any one book or one person's story.

We all have a story to tell. The lessons we gain through our hardest experiences may be exactly what someone else needs to find their way forward.

I think of it as passing a flashlight to someone in the dark. We cannot walk their path for them, but we can help them see the next step. If sharing my story brings hope to even one person, it will have been worth telling. And when others share what they have learned, that light reaches farther.

My hope is to grow T.Y.M.E. into a compassionate, recovery-centered community offering encouragement, connection, and practical resources. Over time, I envision partnerships with qualified professionals and organizations providing counseling, therapy, sober living, and eventually licensed treatment and rehabilitation services.

These are goals for the future. Today, our work begins with books, reflection, and connections to independent resources.

Because your past does not define your future, and today, you mean everything.

William MeyersFounder, T.Y.M.E.
